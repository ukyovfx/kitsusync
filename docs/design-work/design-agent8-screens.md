# Agent 8 — Screen-specific design rules

## Evidence boundary

This is a documentation-only design-system report. It does not change UI code, routes, behavior, or `DESIGN.md`. Current routes and intended purposes are taken from `docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:7-19`. Current renderer facts are from the cited source locations below. Runtime facts are limited to the authenticated pass recorded in `docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:118-140,152-164`; a screen marked `UNTESTED` is not treated as visually confirmed. The connected Production detail state was unavailable, and no setup execution or destructive operation was performed (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:140,155,160`).

The rules below are future design rules, not implementation instructions. Every rule states what to preserve, why it matters, when it applies, and when it must not be used. “Dense” means information-dense but scannable; it does not mean smaller controls. Shared responsive constraints come from `src/setup/ui.go:83-161,285-377` and `docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:82-89`: preserve 44px controls, prevent page-level overflow, allow navigation to scroll at narrow widths, and stack rows/forms/tables when the content requires it.

## Login — `/bot/login`

Current evidence: the live renderer exposes `main`, `form`, one `h1` (`KitsuSync`), visible labels, and 44px inputs/button in both locales; the eight locale/viewport states are captured in `docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:46-54` and `:22-37`. The visible form uses warm dark layered panels and an ambient dot field; the dot animation is confirmed for Login only (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:65-67`).

- User goal: authenticate a Kitsu manager/admin and reach the relevant protected task. First priority: the credential fields, their labels, and the single sign-in action. Primary action: submit Login/ログイン. Allowed secondary info: concise destination/connection guidance and a recovery or language link when relevant; keep it subordinate to authentication. Discouraged patterns: multiple competing CTAs, marketing copy, decorative motion beside the form, or exposing endpoint/credential details. Density: sparse, one-task form. Responsive concern: keep the form readable and controls at least 44px at 375px; preserve the observed no unexpected vertical overflow at 1440×900 (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:51-54`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make authentication the visual and keyboard entry point. WHY: a login page has one decision and errors must be attributable. WHEN: on every unauthenticated login state, including error and discovery states. WHEN NOT: do not reuse the operational dashboard density or place setup management beside the sign-in action.

## Dashboard — `/bot/admin`

Current evidence: the renderer builds attention rows, summary metrics, a New Production Connection CTA, management cards, and activity/status content (`src/setup/ia_views.go:242-331,455-473`). The canonical order is heading/refresh, summary, attention, CTA, management (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:21-35`). The authenticated runtime was reached in both locales and sampled viewports (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:118-120,134,140`).

- User goal: understand whether the system can safely deliver notifications and choose the next administrative task. First priority: readiness and Productions needing attention. Primary action: the context-dependent next action (setup, Connections, or Productions); Refresh status is secondary. Allowed secondary info: connected-Production count, recent failures, compact activity, and links to management areas. Discouraged patterns: treating Production count as Kitsu/Discord health, duplicating the same status in multiple cards, hiding the next action below management tiles, or adding a dashboard-only feature map. Density: medium scan-first summary with compact rows/cards. Responsive concern: keep the five management cards equal-width on desktop, balanced when wrapping, and single-column when necessary; keep Kitsu and Discord rows contained in Connections (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:31-35`).
- Future rule: WHAT: use one ordered decision surface from health to remediation. WHY: operators should know what is blocked before navigating. WHEN: initial dashboard load and any refreshed state. WHEN NOT: do not elevate recent activity above a current blocker or use color alone without a label/hint.

## Productions — `/bot/admin/projects`

Current evidence: each row renders a human-readable Production name, current-state hint, compact status pill, and Open Production action (`src/setup/ia_views.go:476-497`). The status definition is shared with Dashboard: Connected is green, Disconnected is warning yellow, and an unconnected visible Kitsu Production is excluded from the connected count (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:37-41`).

- User goal: find a Production and determine whether it is connected before opening it. First priority: name plus truthful connection state. Primary action: Open Production. Allowed secondary info: a short reason/hint and only the count context needed to explain the state. Discouraged patterns: full-width status bars, neutral gray for Disconnected, stale resource claims, or actions that imply a disconnected Production is ready. Density: compact list, one row per Production. Responsive concern: keep name/state/action aligned on wide screens; stack them at the mobile transition without clipping (`src/setup/ui.go:103-104,158-161`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make status content-sized and semantically explicit. WHY: the operator scans many rows and must compare states quickly. WHEN: every list row and empty/error state. WHEN NOT: do not invent a resource state when the backend has not established it.

## Production detail — `/bot/admin/projects?project=<id>`

Current evidence: the selected view has a named Production heading, status pill, section navigation, and tabs including Overview, Notifications, Users, Activity, Troubleshooting, Details, and Danger Zone (`src/setup/ia_views.go:549-600`). Overview uses four summary concepts plus current issues in the current renderer (`src/setup/ia_views.go:607-716`); the canonical final rules require four equal cards and one counted current-issues card (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:120-135`). Connected detail runtime evidence is explicitly deferred (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:140,155`).

- User goal: operate one selected Production without confusing global configuration, local association, and remote Discord state. First priority: selected Production identity and current connection/readiness. Primary action: the action appropriate to the active tab (review, edit, diagnose, or a narrowly scoped destructive action only after confirmation). Allowed secondary info: real routing/resource state, safe IDs in Details, users/participants distinctions, and localized remediation hints. Discouraged patterns: duplicate or stale resource representations, legacy-renderer links, tabs that imply available capabilities when the Production is disconnected, or mixing global and Production-local state. Density: medium, with a stable header and one focused tab panel. Responsive concern: horizontal tab navigation may scroll; summary cards reduce from four columns to two and then one, and diagnostic grids stack (`src/setup/ui.go:99-101,105-125`).
- Future rule: WHAT: preserve a stable selected-Production context across tabs. WHY: tab changes must not erase which Production is being operated. WHEN: all detail tabs and empty/error states. WHEN NOT: do not show connected-only controls or resource claims when the same connection-state definition says Disconnected (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:39-41`).

## Notifications — Production detail tab

Current evidence: the canonical IA requires a routing status row, editable one-to-one `Kitsu Task Type → Discord Channel` mapping, and a distinct read-only preview that never sends (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:120-128`). Source separates the routing editor and preview and labels the preview as read-only (`src/setup/production_routing.go:325-393`; `src/setup/current_routing.go:265-294`). Disconnected Productions show unavailable/read-only messaging and Task Types rather than pretending delivery is active (`src/setup/ia_views.go:638-647`).

- User goal: verify or safely change where each Task Type notification goes. First priority: whether routing is usable and what destination each mapping resolves to. Primary action: explicit Edit/Apply changes in editor state; in normal state, the primary action is review, not send. Allowed secondary info: selected Task Type, destination, Production notification language, mention behavior, deterministic rendered message/embed, and a dry-run explanation when available. Discouraged patterns: Send/Test controls in preview, implicit saves, ambiguous arrows, mixing routing with remote channel deletion, or showing a successful state for a disconnected Production. Density: medium table/form density with a clear separation between status, routing, and preview. Responsive concern: keep the two mapping columns readable; allow rows/forms to stack or scroll without page overflow (`src/setup/ui.go:113-120,155-158`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make preview read-only and edits explicit. WHY: a preview is evidence for a decision, not a dispatch command. WHEN: normal Notifications view and every preview state. WHEN NOT: do not place a send action in the preview or expose routing controls merely because a preview was selected.

## Users — Production detail tab

Current evidence: the current simple-flow renderer orders `Production users`, `Add a user`, `Assigned`, then `Reviewer / Checker`, with local associations, linked Discord identity, associated status, removal, and separate role assignment (`src/setup/ia_views.go:757-837`). The canonical override excludes bots, keeps Kitsu participant reads diagnostic-only, and requires Production association for Reviewer/Checker eligibility (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:130-141`).

- User goal: manage the human users associated with this Production and assign eligible Reviewer/Checker roles. First priority: who is locally associated and eligible. Primary action: Add a user, then add/remove a role as a separate action. Allowed secondary info: linked Discord identity, associated status, Task Type, and actionable empty-state guidance. Discouraged patterns: presenting Kitsu participants as already managed users, allowing bots or non-associated users into assignment, removing global linking when removing local association, or combining association and role removal into an unclear control. Density: compact linear workflow; avoid a wide administrative table in the normal path. Responsive concern: stack inline forms and row actions at narrow widths while keeping labels and 44px controls usable (`src/setup/ui.go:154,157-160`).
- Future rule: WHAT: keep global User Linking, local Production association, and Reviewer/Checker assignment visually distinct. WHY: they have different scope and different consequences. WHEN: all user lists, add states, role states, and empty states. WHEN NOT: do not show diagnostic participant data in the normal management flow or imply that local removal deletes global identity linking.

## Connections — `/bot/admin/bot`

Current evidence: the runtime confirmed separate named Kitsu and Discord sections in JP/EN (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:126`). The normal IA specifies independent statuses, safe host/token metadata, edit and New Production Connection actions; the edit state has one save action per service and never renders saved secrets (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:43-63`). Edit was step-up gated in the earlier pass but later reached in the post-fix pass; secret fields remained undisplayed (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:127,154`).

- User goal: know which shared service is configured/healthy and update only the needed connection. First priority: independent Kitsu and Discord status plus the safe next action. Primary action: service-specific Edit/Save in the relevant service section; New Production Connection is secondary and contextual. Allowed secondary info: Kitsu host, masked-secret state, saved-secret guidance, and safe service-specific remediation. Discouraged patterns: one blended health badge, showing bot identity as a normal credential row, revealing secrets, or using saved-secret guidance as a health status. Density: medium two-peer-card layout; forms remain service-scoped. Responsive concern: equal-height desktop peers may stack naturally on narrow screens; maintain independent headings and action ownership (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:54-61`).
- Future rule: WHAT: keep Kitsu and Discord health independent. WHY: one service can be healthy while the other is not, and the Dashboard depends on that distinction. WHEN: normal and edit states, including partial configuration. WHEN NOT: do not infer overall notification readiness from one service or collapse the two forms into a single save action.

## User Linking — `/bot/admin/users`

Current evidence: the route purpose is human Kitsu-to-Discord linking (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:14-15`). Source renders a Discord-server selector, linked human-user management, and a separate link form; bot identities are excluded by the current acceptance requirements (`src/setup/ia_views.go:2128-2248`; `docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-ACCEPTANCE.md:58-60`). The runtime confirmed localized JP/EN rendering and the post-fix confirmation dialog for unlink (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:134,152-153`).

- User goal: establish or remove one human Kitsu identity ↔ Discord identity link. First priority: identity pair and current link state. Primary action: explicit Link/Save for a selected pair; unlink is destructive and must enter the confirmation barrier. Allowed secondary info: selected Discord server, human-safe names, current state, and concise scope explanation that Discord identity itself is not changed by unlink. Discouraged patterns: listing bots as people, using color without identity labels, silently unlinking, or mixing Production-local membership into global linking. Density: medium list/form density with one identity pair per row. Responsive concern: convert rows to labeled stacked records and let link controls wrap at narrow widths (`src/setup/ui.go:149,160-161`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make scope and consequence visible next to the action. WHY: global linking and local Production association are easy to confuse. WHEN: link, edit, unlink, and empty states. WHEN NOT: do not use a global link action to assign a Production role or mutate Discord users.

## System Status — `/bot/admin/health`

Current evidence: the page is intended for pipeline health, observations, and recent issues (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:15`). The implementation separates overall health, API response status, operational status, and issues; telemetry refresh is bounded and read-only, with a 5-second in-memory refresh and overlap guard (`src/setup/ia_views.go:1338-1455`; `src/setup/runtime_observation.go`; `src/main.go:875`). Canonical chart rules require independent zero-based scales, timestamp-positioned observations, exact localized labels, safe tooltips, and no fabricated latency (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:147-155`). Runtime charts/status cards were rendered, but chart keyboard and reduced-motion protected-state evidence remain limited (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:140,156-157`).

- User goal: decide whether KitsuSync is healthy and identify the smallest safe next diagnosis. First priority: overall status and current Kitsu/Discord response state. Primary action: inspect the surfaced remediation/Details; selector changes the read-only observation window. Allowed secondary info: separate Kitsu/Discord cards, current value, one local-time Last updated line, real operational details, chronological charts, and recent issues only when present. Discouraged patterns: one blended API metric, fixed latency thresholds, fabricated values, normal-card telemetry clutter, or treating functional polling as decoration. Density: high but layered: headline status first, peer API cards second, expandable details/issues last. Responsive concern: keep peer cards and plot regions equal on desktop; stack cards and preserve readable labels, axes, tooltips, and no horizontal overflow on mobile (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:146-155`; `src/setup/ui.go:137,158`).
- Future rule: WHAT: show evidence before diagnosis. WHY: operators need to distinguish process state, reachability, and semantic health. WHEN: every healthy, failed, empty, stale, and refresh-error state. WHEN NOT: do not convert missing observations into healthy values or expose credentials, headers, bodies, secret URLs, or internal IDs.

## Audit Log — `/bot/admin/audit`

Current evidence: the route purpose is operation and notification history (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:16`). Dashboard activity currently shows date/time, action, Production, and result from recent audit records (`src/setup/ia_views.go:266-287,321-323`); the full handler renders up to 200 records (`src/setup/admin.go:3460-3497`). Audit timestamps must use the same viewer-local conversion and IANA timezone context as System Status (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:151-153`). Runtime reached an empty Audit Log state; populated behavior was not evidenced (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:140`).

- User goal: reconstruct what operation or notification event occurred and whether it succeeded. First priority: timestamp, action, Production context, and result. Primary action: inspect/read the record; filtering or navigation is secondary if present. Allowed secondary info: safe event type, localized result, actor/context only when non-secret and meaningful, and explicit empty state. Discouraged patterns: turning logs into a live dashboard, hiding failures among decoration, exposing raw payloads/headers/tokens, or showing timestamps without timezone context. Density: high, chronological rows with stable columns and compact metadata. Responsive concern: preserve event/action/result association; stack or label columns at narrow widths rather than forcing page overflow (`src/setup/ui.go:147-161`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make each event self-identifying without opening a secret-bearing detail surface. WHY: audit is for accountability, not raw debugging dumps. WHEN: populated, empty, and failure-heavy logs. WHEN NOT: do not render credential values, authorization data, response bodies, or unnecessary internal identifiers.

## Setup Wizard — `/bot/setup`

Current evidence: `/bot/setup` is the primary operator setup page; the documented sequence covers login, dedicated Kitsu runtime, Discord, Production destination setup, and Health verification (`docs/SETUP_WIZARD.md:8-20,58-108`). The renderer has four step indicators and separate prerequisite, Production, server, plan, review, execution, complete, and error states (`src/setup/ia_views.go:2438-2860`; `src/setup/first_time_connection.go:245-273`). Setup execution was not performed in runtime evidence (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:70-72,140`).

- User goal: connect one Production through a predictable, reviewable sequence. First priority: current step, prerequisites, and the next safe decision. Primary action: continue to the next step; on the review step, explicit Execute Setup only after the complete plan is understood. Allowed secondary info: prerequisite status, selected Production/server, planned category/channels/routes, conflict/reuse explanation, rollback/retry outcome, and a route to Health after completion. Discouraged patterns: hiding writes behind generic Save, presenting execution before review, mixing shared Connections with Production-specific plan choices, or claiming completion before verification. Density: progressive disclosure—sparse step framing, denser review plan, focused result/recovery. Responsive concern: four-step navigation must remain legible at 375px; plan tables must wrap/scroll safely and controls stack (`src/setup/ui.go:158,485-488`).
- Future rule: WHAT: make the wizard’s irreversible boundary explicit and reviewable. WHY: setup can create remote Discord resources and must support recovery. WHEN: before execution, after partial failure, and on retry. WHEN NOT: do not auto-submit, skip the review plan, or describe rollback as complete unless the result proves it.

## Diagnostics / Troubleshooting — `/bot/admin/health` and Production detail `troubleshooting`

Current evidence: `/bot/admin/diagnostics` redirects to System Status, while Production detail has a dedicated Troubleshooting tab (`src/main.go:932-936`; `src/setup/ia_views.go:600-658,1159-1314`). The documented scope is compact, real diagnostics for Kitsu, Discord, routing integrity, participant retrieval, User Linking, and recent notification processing; Details is read-only and localized (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:126-128`). The setup diagnostic model carries environment, Kitsu, Discord, project, test-notification, and completion states (`src/setup/ux_setup.go:34-55,204-234`).

- User goal: explain why a real operation is unavailable or unsafe and point to the smallest corrective surface. First priority: diagnosis plus next action. Primary action: open the named remediation surface or inspect safe Details; diagnostic checks themselves remain read-only unless explicitly labeled otherwise. Allowed secondary info: check status, evidence summary, routing/participant/linking integrity, processing state, safe counts, and recent notification outcome. Discouraged patterns: generic “something went wrong,” speculative causes, raw response dumps, credentials, or duplicating System Status as another dashboard. Density: compact diagnostic grid with expandable detail; one issue per row/card. Responsive concern: two-column diagnostic groups reduce to one column at narrow widths; long identifiers wrap (`src/setup/ui.go:121-125,150-151`).
- Future rule: WHAT: pair every failed check with evidence and one bounded next action. WHY: diagnosis is useful only when it reduces uncertainty. WHEN: warning, error, unavailable, and incomplete states. WHEN NOT: do not claim a check passed from HTTP 200 alone, and do not expose secret-bearing diagnostics.

## Danger Zone — Production detail `danger-zone`

Current evidence: Danger Zone is a collapsed details section containing separate “disconnect Production” and “delete Discord resources” actions; the source states their different scopes and uses confirmation plus required confirmation text (`src/setup/ia_views.go:1314-1320`). Connected-resource deletion has additional validation/review flows in `src/setup/admin.go:1328-1647`; the runtime pass did not execute destructive actions (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:140,160`).

- User goal: intentionally perform a clearly understood destructive or high-impact Production action. First priority: action scope, consequence, and recovery/re-setup path. Primary action: none by default; the user must first expand the zone, review the exact action, then confirm the selected operation. Allowed secondary info: what is and is not removed, validation result, required confirmation text, and post-action/retry guidance. Discouraged patterns: prominent destructive CTA on page load, combining disconnect with Discord deletion, vague “Delete” labels, preselected confirmation text, or hiding recoverability. Density: intentionally sparse and separated from routine controls. Responsive concern: stack danger action blocks and confirmation controls; keep the full warning and action scope readable at narrow widths (`src/setup/ui.go:147,152-158`).
- Future rule: WHAT: preserve a two-stage barrier—scope review, then explicit confirmation. WHY: disconnecting KitsuSync and deleting Discord resources are materially different operations. WHEN: every destructive action, including channel/resource cleanup and unlink/removal equivalents. WHEN NOT: do not bypass confirmation for a row action, treat a validation preview as deletion, or imply that one action performs the other.

## Cross-screen invariants

- WHAT: maintain JP/EN semantic parity, including state, order, action scope, and empty/error guidance. WHY: runtime confirms basic localized rendering, but interaction-level parity still needs focused verification (`docs/UI-UX-RUNTIME-EVIDENCE.md:134`). WHEN: every screen and responsive state. WHEN NOT: do not translate labels while leaving a hidden English status or changing the action meaning.
- WHAT: use status words and color together, with `Connected/接続済`, `Disconnected/未接続`, `Needs review/要確認`, `Error/エラー`, `Healthy/正常`, and `Unavailable/利用不可` retaining their semantic roles. WHY: color alone is insufficient and the vocabulary is already canonical (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:65-80`). WHEN: badges, rows, summaries, charts, and empty/error states. WHEN NOT: do not use warm accent, green, yellow, or red decoratively as a substitute for state.
- WHAT: never render secrets or raw sensitive diagnostics. WHY: the source and acceptance rules explicitly require masked/undisplayed credentials and secret-safe observability (`docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-SPEC.md:60,113-118,147-155`; `docs/CURRENT-IA-UI-ACCEPTANCE.md:103-110`). WHEN: Connections, System Status, Audit Log, Diagnostics, Setup results, previews, and confirmations. WHEN NOT: do not include secret contents in visible UI, tooltips, logs, telemetry, or report evidence.
